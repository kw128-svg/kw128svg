### Abstract
Conventional Helmholtz coils suffer from a large physical footprint, which limits the integration and miniaturization of atom-based magnetometry systems. In this work, we design compact, high-uniformity biplanar magnetic coils by formulating the magnetic-field synthesis problem as a least-squares approximation in an inner-product space and constructing a stream-function representation of the current distribution based on Maxwell's equations.

To overcome the ill-conditioned and singular solutions commonly encountered in conventional target-field approaches, we establish an analytical basis-function framework derived from the Sturm–Liouville problem. Using MATLAB numerical simulations, Ising-model quantization, and particle-swarm optimization (PSO), we obtain experimentally realizable coil geometries. Square and circular prototype coils were fabricated and experimentally characterized using an atomic magnetometer, demonstrating significantly improved magnetic-field uniformity within a compact volume.

### Key Contributions
* **Analytical Framework:** Formulated a Sturm–Liouville stream-function basis expansion to regularize ill-conditioned target-field inverse problems.
* **Numerical Optimization:** Combined Ising-model discrete quantization with particle-swarm optimization (PSO) to generate practical, continuous wire trajectories.
* **Experimental Validation:** Fabricated square and circular biplanar coils and verified their spatial magnetic-field uniformity using an atomic magnetometer.
